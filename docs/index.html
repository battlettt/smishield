smishield

An SMS smishing & spam detector that runs entirely in your browser.

Your message never leaves this device. The model is downloaded once with this page and every message is analysed locally by JavaScript in this tab. Nothing you paste is sent to a server, stored, or logged.

        Limitations: please read

        • 2012-era data. The model learned from the SMS Spam Collection, a public dataset of messages collected around 2011–2012. Modern smishing (fake parcel fees, bank & toll alerts, shortened links) looks different, so the model can miss it. The pattern checks help explain those cases but don't change the score.
        • English only. The training messages are English (mostly UK and Singapore). Other languages will not be scored reliably.
        • A demo and learning project. It is not a substitute for your carrier's spam filtering or your phone's built-in protections, and a "likely legitimate" result is not a guarantee of safety.
        • If a message asks you to tap a link, pay, or log in, go to the organisation's site or app directly instead. You can report spam texts to your carrier (in the US and UK, forward them to 7726).

        How it works

        1. The message is lowercased and split into words. Links, currency symbols and long numbers (phone numbers, short codes) are replaced by placeholder tokens, and adjacent word pairs (bigrams) are added.
        2. Each word and pair is weighted by TF-IDF using the vocabulary and IDF values learned during training.
        3. A logistic regression model combines the weights into a spam probability; 50% or above is flagged as spam, the same rule used at training time.

        The model was trained in Python with scikit-learn and exported to model.json; the scoring is reimplemented in plain TypeScript with no machine-learning runtime. Metrics: metrics.json.

        Dataset: T. A. Almeida, J. M. Gómez Hidalgo & A. Yamakami, SMS Spam Collection v.1, UCI Machine Learning Repository.
